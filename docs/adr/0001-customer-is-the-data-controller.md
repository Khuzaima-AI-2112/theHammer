# The Customer is the data controller; theHammer is a processor

theHammer captures screenshots of identifiable employees and reports their idle
time to their employer, which is workplace monitoring. We decided that each
Customer organisation is the party responsible for that monitoring, and
theHammer acts only as their supplier: the Customer decides to monitor, tells
its own staff, and answers its own employees' requests about their data.

## Considered options

The alternative was for theHammer to take responsibility for every Monitored
User across every Workspace. We rejected it because only an employer has the
employment relationship that makes monitoring lawful, and because it would make
Chris liable for the staff of every company that signs up.

## Consequences

theHammer must *supply the mechanism* even though it does not own the
obligation: a Disclosure screen, Restricted Mode until Acknowledgement, a
server-side Acknowledgement record the Customer can produce as evidence, and a
written agreement assigning responsibility to the Customer. Product work does
not disappear under this decision — it changes owner.
